1The massage

Most hot tubs put a thumb over the hose.

Skinny plumbing squeezes the water down, like your thumb over the end of a garden hose. It comes out fast and sharp.

That itchy feeling isn’t a massage.

A thin, high-pressure jet stings the skin instead of working the muscle. Most people feel it as an itch, and they stop using the jets.

So we open up the hose.

AquaSolus uses wide plumbing that delivers four to five times more water to every jet. A big, warm volume of water works over a larger area of your back, gets the blood moving through tight muscles and draws the tension out.

Think of your shower.

A skinny showerhead never makes a good shower. A big rain head does. Hydrotherapy works the same way.

2The water flow

All that water has to go somewhere.

Every drop that comes out of a jet goes back through the filter, the double ozonator and the heater before it comes back to you.

A typical hot tub takes about 45 minutes.

That’s how long, on average, it takes to move all of its water through the filter and ozone. That slow cycle is why so many owners rely on so many chemicals.

An AquaSolus does it in under 10.

With four to five times more water moving, every drop is filtered and sanitized more than four times faster. Cleaner water, fewer chemicals, less to think about.

3The double ozonator

Every AquaSolus has a double ozonator.

Two stages of sanitizing, standard in every series. Every hot tub has an ozonator. What matters is what happens inside it.

Stage one: the mixing chamber.

The ozone is held under water for at least 45 seconds, so it actually works on the water instead of bubbling away.

Breaking down what grows in water.

While the ozone and water mix, it breaks down bacteria, yeast, mould, algae and biofilm.

Stage two: UV. Then back to you, clear.

The water passes a UV stage, then the heater, then out through the jets. Clean, soft water you’ll want to sit in every night.

4The energy

We use 56-frame pumps, not 48-frame.

Here’s why that matters to you, not just to the spec sheet.

Skinny plumbing builds pressure in the lines.

A 48-frame pump on skinny plumbing is forcing water through a narrow pipe. That builds pressure inside the lines, and the pump has to work against it every time it runs. A 56-frame pump on wide plumbing lets the water move freely.

Working against that pressure costs you.

A pump fighting pressure in the lines uses more power, so it costs more to run. It also wears out faster, so it fails sooner and has to be replaced. A pump that isn’t fighting runs for less and lasts longer.

Most hot tub makers cut corners where you can’t see.

Skinny plumbing and a 48-frame pump cost less to build. Many dealers like it too: you’re back sooner for a new pump, and buying more chemicals the whole time you own the tub. That’s not Zen.

5The insulation

Efficient on day one is easy. Efficient in year ten is Zen.

How a hot tub is insulated, and where, decides what it costs you to own long after the delivery truck leaves.

Full foam sounds good. Until something needs fixing.

Many hot tubs fill the cabinet with spray foam. If a fitting leaks years later, a technician has to dig through wet, hardened foam to find it. It never goes back the way it was, and the cold spots it leaves show up on your hydro bill.

And the heart of the tub is left out in the cold.

Full-foam tubs don’t foam around the pumps, the heater or the control box. Insulation is only as good as its weakest point, and that’s where the heat gets out. On a cold night the water in the lines around that equipment isn’t insulated at all, so the tub keeps switching on to move water. That isn’t energy efficient.

AQ10 insulates all of it. 100% of the water, 100% of the time.

AquaSolus insulates the perimeter instead: the cabinet walls, the shell and a sealed ABS base, with the pumps, heater and every line inside that envelope. Everything stays clean, dry and easy to reach, so a service call never costs you your insulation.

Free heat, put back to work.

Your pumps make warmth every time they run. In a full-foam tub that heat escapes out of the bare equipment bay. Inside the AQ10 envelope it stays in the cabinet and helps keep your water warm, so the heater does less. All on a 304 stainless steel frame that won’t rot or rust.

Sealed on all six sides.

The cover holds the heat at the surface. AQ10 wraps the walls and shell. The ABS base seals the bottom against cold, damp ground and critters. Lower bills, easy service and one less thing to think about. That’s Zen.
