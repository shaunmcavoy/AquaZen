1Efficiency

Peace of mind is part of Zen.

Zen isn’t only what you feel in the water. It’s opening the hydro bill without a knot in your stomach, and knowing the tub will keep running quietly for years.

It starts with the pumps.

The pumps are the heart of a hot tub. They drive every jet and move every drop through the filter and heater. Most hot tubs use smaller 48-frame pumps. We use 56-frame pumps, not 48-frame, and that one choice shapes everything that follows.

Smaller pumps go with smaller plumbing.

A 48-frame pump on smaller plumbing is forcing water through a narrow pipe. That builds pressure inside the lines, and the pump has to work against it every time it runs. A 56-frame pump on bigger plumbing lets the water move freely.

Working against that pressure costs you.

A pump fighting pressure in the lines uses more power, so it costs more to run. It also wears out faster, so it fails sooner and has to be replaced. A pump that isn’t fighting runs for less and lasts longer.

Why every tub isn’t built this way.

Smaller plumbing and a smaller pump cost less to build, and a tub that needs more chemicals and more service keeps a store busy. We’d rather give you the best options from day one, so you’re the one who stays busy, enjoying it.

2The massage

Remember the garden hose?

When you were a kid and wanted to spray your friends, you put your thumb over the end. Less water came out, but it came out fast and sharp.

Most hot tubs do the same thing.

Remember those smaller pumps and smaller plumbing? That’s the thumb. Entry-level and mid-level hot tubs squeeze the water through narrow lines on its way from the pumps to the jets, so you get higher pressure and less water.

That’s why the jets feel itchy.

So we take the thumb off the hose.

Now it actually feels amazing.

A big, warm volume of water works over a larger area of your back. It gets the blood and oxygen moving through tight muscles, and the comfortable pressure of all that water reaches deep to release the tension.

3Clean, easy water

Lift the cover. See the seats.

You want water so clear you can see right to the bottom, that feels soft on your skin and doesn’t smell like chemicals. And you don’t want to spend your evenings testing it.

All that extra water has to go somewhere.

Every drop that comes out of a jet goes back through the tub’s cleaning system before it comes back to you. How fast it gets there decides how clean your water stays.

A typical hot tub takes about 45 minutes.

That’s how long, on average, it takes to move all of its water through the filter and ozone. With smaller pumps and smaller plumbing, the water just doesn’t move fast enough. That slow cycle is why so many owners rely on so many chemicals.

An AquaSolus does it in under 10.

With four to five times more water moving, all of it gets cleaned more than four times faster. Here’s what happens on the way.

First, the filter.

It catches what we all bring into the water: hair, dirt, lotion and body oils. The more often the water passes through, the less of it hangs around.

Then the best part: the double ozonator.

Every hot tub has an ozonator. What matters is what happens inside it, and how much water actually gets through it. Every AquaSolus has two stages, standard in every series.

Stage one: the mixing chamber.

The ozone is held under water for at least 45 seconds, so it actually works on the water instead of bubbling away.

Breaking down what grows in water.

While the ozone and water mix, it breaks down bacteria, yeast, mould, algae and biofilm.

Stage two: UV. Then back to you, clear.

The water passes a UV stage, then the heater, then out through the jets. Clean, soft water you’ll want to sit in every night.

Why smaller plumbing misses out.

A hot tub built on smaller pumps and smaller plumbing can have an ozonator too. But when its water takes about 45 minutes to get around, most of it is waiting its turn, so it never gets the full benefit. The technology is only as good as the water that reaches it.

4Heat control

The coldest night in January.

Snow on the deck, steam rising off the water. It’s the best soak of the year, and it’s when a hot tub shows how it was built.

Efficient on day one is easy. Efficient in year ten is Zen.

How a hot tub is insulated, and where, decides what it costs you to own long after the delivery truck leaves.

Full foam sounds good. Until something needs fixing.

Many hot tubs fill the cabinet with spray foam. If a fitting leaks years later, a technician has to dig through wet, hardened foam to find it. It never goes back the way it was, and the cold spots it leaves show up on your hydro bill.

And the heart of the tub is left out in the cold.

Full-foam tubs don’t foam around the pumps, the heater or the control box. Insulation is only as good as its weakest point, and that’s where the heat gets out. On a cold night the water in the lines around that equipment isn’t insulated at all, so the tub keeps switching on to move water.

AQ10 insulates all of it. 100% of the water, 100% of the time.

AquaSolus insulates the perimeter instead: the cabinet walls, the shell and a sealed ABS base, with the pumps, heater and every line inside that envelope. Everything stays clean, dry and easy to reach, so a service call never costs you your insulation.

Free heat, put back to work.

Your pumps make warmth every time they run. In a full-foam tub that heat escapes out of the bare equipment bay. Inside the AQ10 envelope it stays in the cabinet and helps keep your water warm, so the heater does less. All on a 304 stainless steel frame that won’t rot or rust.

Sealed on all six sides.

The cover holds the heat at the surface. AQ10 wraps the walls and shell. The ABS base seals the bottom against cold, damp ground and critters. Lower bills, easy service and one less thing to think about. That’s Zen.
