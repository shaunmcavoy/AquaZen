Owner’s Manual02

Your tub

How your tub looks after the water

Every drop that comes out of a jet goes back through the filter, the double ozonator and the heater before it comes back to you. That’s why your part is only a few minutes a week.

Filtration

Water cleaned in under 10 minutes

Your tub turns all of its water over through the filter and ozone in under 10 minutes. A typical hot tub takes about 45. Faster turnover means clearer water with less help from chemicals.

Double ozonator

A 45-second mixing chamber, then UV

Ozone is held under water for at least 45 seconds so it works on the water instead of bubbling away. It breaks down bacteria, yeast, mould, algae and biofilm. Then the water passes a UV stage on its way back to you.

AQ10 insulation

100% of the water, 100% of the time

The pumps, heater and every line sit inside the insulated cabinet, sealed underneath by an ABS base. Heat from the pumps stays in to help warm the water, so the heater does less. And it all stays dry and easy to reach for service.

Pumps

56-frame pumps, not 48-frame

Big, unstrained pumps move a large volume of warm water through wide plumbing. That’s the deep, soft massage you feel, and the flow that keeps your water moving through the filter.

Important Ozone and UV make your sanitizer’s job much easier, but they don’t replace it. Always keep chlorine in the water. Because they do so much, a teaspoon after each soak is all it takes.
Owner’s Manual03

Getting started

Filling and start-up

We fill and start your tub on delivery day. Use these steps every time you refill after a water change. Doing it in this order pushes air out of the pumps and lines, so everything starts smoothly.

Before you fill

  1. Make sure the power is off at the disconnect.
  2. Lift out the filter cartridges. The number varies by model.
  3. Wipe the shell clean with a hot tub surface cleaner.
  4. Check that the drain valve is fully closed.
  5. Turn every air control on the top of the tub to off. Left open, they slow heating a lot.
  6. Give each jet a quarter turn to make sure it’s open.

Fill

  1. Run your garden hose on the lawn for 5 minutes to flush out stale water.
  2. Put the hose in the filter area. This fills the pumps and lines first and pushes the air out.
  3. Fill to the minimum water level line. No line? Fill to about 2.5 cm (1 in) above the top of the highest back jets.
  4. Check for drips under the side panels. Hand-tighten any loose union. Never use a wrench.

Start-up

  1. Turn the power on. Run each jet pump a few times to push out any trapped air.
  2. Put the clean filters back in.
  3. Set your temperature and close the cover. Heating usually takes 12 to 24 hours. Series One on 120V takes longer.
  4. Balance the water first (page 5), then add chlorine.
Air lock?

If a pump hums but the water won’t move, turn that jet pump on and off about 10 times. Still stuck? Call or text us.

Important Never run the tub empty or below the minimum line, and never without its filters. Don’t soak until the water is balanced and sanitized.
Top tip. Top up the water at least once a week. Low water starves the pumps and heater.
Owner’s Manual04

Getting started

Balancing our soft water

Lower Mainland water is naturally soft and acidic. Great for your kettle, but in a hot tub it can be hard on the heater and jets, and it foams easily. A little balancing on each fresh fill keeps the water gentle on you and on your tub.

On every fresh fill, in this order

  1. Total alkalinity first. Ours usually starts low. Raise it into range with alkalinity increaser. Alkalinity holds your pH steady.
  2. Then pH. Once alkalinity is right, pH is much easier to set. Adjust with pH up or down.
  3. Then calcium hardness. Soft water wants to pull calcium from wherever it can find it. Adding calcium hardness increaser protects the heater and jets and helps stop foaming.
  4. Then chlorine. Bring free chlorine up into range with chlorine granules, added to the filter area with the jets running. Never let granules sit on the shell. They can bleach or blister it.

Add one product at a time with the jets running, wait the time on the label, then retest.

Know your numbers
TestAim for
Free chlorine3 to 5 ppm
pH7.2 to 7.6
Total alkalinity80 to 120 ppm
Calcium hardness150 to 250 ppm
Water temperature40°C (104°F) max

Chlorine, pH, alkalinity and temperature from HealthLinkBC File 27b. Calcium from common spa guidance. Your test kit and product labels have the final word.1,2

Just one teaspoon

Your double ozonator is so effective that the only sanitizer you’ll add is 1 teaspoon (5 mL) of chlorine granules after each soak, with the jets running. We don’t recommend bromine in our tubs. Chlorine is all your AquaSolus needs.

Owner’s Manual07

Routine

Your routine at a glance

This is the whole job. A couple of minutes before you soak, ten minutes once a week, and a few bigger jobs through the year.

Before every soak
  • Dip a test strip. Chlorine and pH in range? In you go.
  • Rinse off first. Lotions, makeup and deodorant cloud water fastest.
After every soak
  • Add 1 teaspoon (5 mL) of chlorine granules to the filter area with the jets running. That’s all the sanitizer your tub needs.
  • Put the cover on and lock it. It keeps heat in, rain and leaves out, and little ones safe.
Every week
  • Test chlorine, pH and alkalinity. Adjust alkalinity first, then pH.
  • Rinse the filter (page 9).
  • Top up the water and wipe the waterline.
  • Check the water even in weeks you don’t soak.
Every month
  • Test calcium hardness and top it up if it’s low.
  • Look over the cover and under the side panels for drips or wear.
Every 3 to 4 months
  • Give the filter an overnight soak in filter cleaner.
  • Drain, clean and refill with fresh water (page 10).
Every 6 months
  • Replace your filters. AquaSolus recommends every 26 weeks.
Every year
  • Book a service visit. AquaSolus recommends at least one a year. Send a request from your portal.
Owner’s Manual08

Filters

Filter care

With water turning over in under 10 minutes, your filter catches a lot. A clean filter is the single biggest thing you can do for clear water, and it’s what keeps most control panel messages away.

Every week: rinse

  1. Turn the power off at the disconnect, or use your panel’s standby or maintenance setting.
  2. Lift out the filter and hose it off, working the spray between every pleat from top to bottom.
  3. Spread the pleats, spray with a quick filter cleaner and wait about 20 minutes.
  4. Rinse thoroughly so no cleaner goes back into your water.
  5. Put it back, restore power and check the jets run.

A new fill or a busy week can clog a filter in three or four days. If the jets feel weaker, rinse sooner. Clean it at least every two weeks no matter what.

Every 3 months: deep soak

Soak the filter overnight in filter cleaner mixed as the label says, rinse well and let it dry completely before it goes back in. Your water change is the perfect time.

Every 6 months

Replace your filters

Sooner if the pleats are torn or flattened, the end caps are cracked, or the filter still looks grey or greasy after a deep clean. Get replacements from us so they’re the right fit for your model.

Top tip

Keep a spare set

Swap in a clean, dry set while you soak the other, and your tub is never without filtration. AquaSolus suggests alternating them at each water change.

Important Never run your tub without its filters while it has water in it.

Owner’s Manual09

Seasonal care

Water changes, cover and winter

Fresh water every few months is the easiest way to keep things simple. Plan on every 3 to 4 months, sooner if the tub gets a lot of use or the water starts to feel tired.

Changing the water

  1. With the tub still full, add a spa line flush and run the jets for 20 to 30 minutes. Foam means it’s working.
  2. Turn the power off at the disconnect.
  3. Open the drain valve and attach a garden hose, or use a submersible pump. It can take a couple of hours.
  4. Lift out the filters and give them their overnight soak.
  5. Wipe the shell with a hot tub surface cleaner. Never household cleaners, which can foam and damage the acrylic.
  6. Close the drain and refill through the filter area, just like your first fill (page 4). Then balance and sanitize (page 5).
Your cover
  • Keep it on and locked whenever you’re not soaking.
  • Brush off water, leaves and snow so it stays light and seals well.
  • Wipe the underside when you change the water.
  • Don’t sit, stand or put things on it.
Cold snaps
  • Keep the power on and the tub running. Every line sits inside the AQ10 insulation, so it isn’t working hard to stay warm.
  • Never leave a tub empty, or full and switched off, in freezing weather. Water left in the lines can freeze and crack them.
  • Need a winter water change? Refill right away, or call us first.
  • Going away or shutting down for the season? Call us. A tub has to be properly winterized, not just drained.
Owner’s Manual10

For everyone

Safe soaking

Zen Time should only ever feel good. A few simple habits keep it that way for everyone who uses your tub.

In the water

  • Never go above 40°C (104°F). Most people love it between 37 and 40°C (100 and 104°F). Start lower and nudge it up.
  • Use a lower temperature and shorter soaks for children, and always watch them closely.
  • If you’re pregnant, or have heart, blood pressure, circulation or diabetes concerns, check with your doctor before soaking.
  • Skip the tub after hard exercise, or with alcohol, drugs or medication that makes you drowsy.
  • Feel dizzy, sleepy or too warm? Get out slowly and cool down.
  • Keep long hair away from the suction covers, and never use the tub if a cover is broken or missing.
Handling chemicals
  • Hot tub chemicals only. Never pool chemicals.
  • Never mix products together, even in the same scoop.
  • Add one at a time with the jets running, as the label directs.
  • Store them cool, dry, closed and out of reach of children.
  • Not sure about a product? Ask us first. It’s an easy way to protect your warranty.
Around the tub
  • Lock the cover every time. It keeps children and pets safe.
  • Keep electrical appliances at least 1.5 m (5 ft) away, unless they’re built into the tub.
  • Use non-slip steps and keep the path clear and lit.
