1The massage

Most hot tubs put a thumb over the hose.

Skinny plumbing squeezes the water down, like your thumb over the end of a garden hose. It comes out fast and sharp.

That itchy feeling isn’t a massage.

A thin, high-pressure jet stings the skin instead of working the muscle. Most people feel it as an itch, and they stop using the jets.

So we open up the hose.

Aquasolus uses wide plumbing that delivers four to five times more water to every jet. A big, warm volume of water works over a larger area of your back, gets the blood moving through tight muscles and draws the tension out.

Think of your shower.

A skinny showerhead never makes a good shower. A big rain head does. Hydrotherapy works the same way.

2The water flow

All that water has to go somewhere.

Every drop that comes out of a jet goes back through the filter, the double ozonator and the heater before it comes back to you.

A typical hot tub takes about 45 minutes.

That’s how long, on average, it takes to move all of its water through the filter and ozone. That slow cycle is why so many owners rely on so many chemicals.

An Aquasolus does it in under 10.

With four to five times more water moving, every drop is filtered and sanitized more than four times faster. Cleaner water, fewer chemicals, less to think about.

3The double ozonator

Every Aquasolus has a double ozonator.

Two stages of sanitizing, standard in every series. Every hot tub has an ozonator. What matters is what happens inside it.

Stage one: the mixing chamber.

The ozone is held under water for at least 45 seconds, so it actually works on the water instead of bubbling away.

Breaking down what grows in water.

While the ozone and water mix, it breaks down bacteria, yeast, mould, algae and biofilm.

Stage two: UV. Then back to you, clear.

The water passes a UV stage, then the heater, then out through the jets. Clean, soft water you’ll want to sit in every night.

4The energy

We use 56-frame pumps, not 48-frame.

Here’s why that matters to you, not just to the spec sheet.

A bigger wet end moves more water with less effort.

The 56-frame pump’s larger wet end, matched to wide plumbing, lets the water move freely. A 48-frame pump on skinny lines has to push against back pressure every time it runs.

Less strain. Lower bills. Longer life.

A pump that isn’t straining draws less electricity and lasts longer. That’s money back in your pocket and fewer service calls.

And the heat stays in.

Full AQ10 insulation wraps the shell, cabinet and base. The heat the pumps make while they run goes back into the water instead of into the air. Easy on the planet, and on your hydro bill.
